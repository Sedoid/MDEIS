# Baobab College Evaluation Methodology

**Document ID:** BAO-METH-1.0 | **Version:** 1.0 | **Effective:** school year 2027 | **Review:** end of each school year

**Status.** Baobab College is a fictional school. This document is a synthetic test fixture. It has no real school, teacher or learner in it. The scale, the bands and every number in it are invented for testing.

---

## 1. Purpose

This document says how Baobab College reads school records over time. It describes what the records show for one learner in one subject. It does not decide anything about a learner. It does not name a trait for a learner, and it does not rank or compare learners.

A named teacher approves every report before it leaves the system. Every finding names the records and the rule numbers it came from.

## 2. Principles

- Describe the data. Say "the Mathematics scores moved up by 11 points over three terms". Never describe a learner by a trait.
- Compare a learner only with the same learner's own earlier scores in the same subject. Never compare one learner with another.
- Never average scores. No average of any kind is made in this document.
- Never suggest a subject choice, a pathway or a career.
- Say plainly when there is not enough evidence.
- Numbers come from the rules in section 6 and from nowhere else.

## 4. Evaluation process

Every report follows the steps below. The rules in section 6 are applied when the evidence is complete as set out in section 5. The steps are:

1. Name the learner, the subject and the report period.
2. Confirm the settings in section 3 and that a named teacher is signed in as the approver.
3. Collect the term scores recorded for the subject.
4. Validate each score. A score outside 0 to 100, or with no date, is not used and goes to the teacher as a question.
5. Count the scores in the window. If there are fewer than 2, state that there is not enough evidence and stop.
6. Work out the band, the spread and the change, as set out in sections 6.2, 6.3 and 6.4.
7. Write the findings in plain words. Each finding names its record IDs and rule numbers.
8. Send the draft to the named teacher for approval.

## 5. Records and evidence

- **Eligible record:** one written test score for each subject at the end of each term, out of 100.
- **What a record keeps:** its ID, the learner ID, the subject, the term, the date and the score.
- **Missing term:** a term with no score is "not recorded". It is never counted as zero.
- **Invalid score:** a score outside 0 to 100, or with no date, is not used. It goes to the teacher as a question.
- **Window:** the last 3 term scores recorded in that subject, in date order. When more than 3 exist, only the last 3 are used.
- **Minimum evidence:** at least 2 term scores in the window. With 2 scores the measures run on those 2 and the report says it is based on 2 scores. With 1 score, or none, no pattern is stated.

## 6. Calculation rules

Three measures are worked out for one learner in one subject. They use only that learner's own term scores. No score is averaged.

### 6.1 Grade bands

The grade bands are set by the school board and listed in section 3.2.

| Band | Score range |
| --- | --- |
| A | 80 to 100 |
| B | 70 to 79 |
| C | 60 to 69 |
| D | 50 to 59 |
| E | 0 to 49 |

A band names a score range. It names a score range and says nothing about the learner.

### 6.2 Band of a score

**Inputs.** One term score, and the grade bands in section 3.2. The measure uses nothing else.

**Definition.** Band = the band whose score range holds the score, with both ends of the range included.

**Source of the numbers.** The bands are set by the school board and listed in section 3.2. The pass mark of 50 is set by the school board too. A score meets the pass mark when it is 50 or higher.

**Worked example.** A score of 74 falls in band B, because 74 is between 70 and 79. A score of 50 falls in band D and meets the pass mark. A score of 49 falls in band E and does not meet it.

### 6.3 Spread and the steady rule

**Inputs.** The scores in the window (section 5) for one learner in one subject.

**Definition.** Spread = the highest score minus the lowest score in the window. The pattern is steady when the spread is at most 8 points. A spread of 8 counts as steady.

**Source of the numbers.** The steady band of 8 points is set by the school board and listed in section 3.2.

**Worked example.** The scores, oldest first, were 61, 64 and 58. The highest score was 64 and the lowest was 58. The spread was 6, which is at most 8, so the pattern is steady. A second window, 55, 70 and 64, has a spread of 15, so it is not steady.

### 6.4 Change and the moved rule

**Inputs.** The first score and the last score in the window, in date order.

**Definition.** Change = the last score minus the first score. The pattern is moved up when the change is 10 points or more. It is moved down when the change is -10 points or less. Otherwise it is not moved. A pattern cannot be both steady and moved, because a spread of 8 or less gives a change of 8 or less. When a window is neither steady nor moved, no pattern is named and the scores are shown with no pattern name.

**Source of the numbers.** The moved threshold of 10 points is set by the school board and listed in section 3.2.

**Worked example.** The scores, oldest first, were 52, 58 and 63. The change was 63 minus 52, which is 11, so the pattern is moved up. A second window, 70, 66 and 62, has a change of -8, so it is not moved.

## 7. Reporting and approval

- **Order:** the scores with their bands first, then the pattern, then the limits of the evidence.
- **Wording:** describe the records. For example, "the Mathematics scores moved up by 11 points over three terms (records R-0001 to R-0003, rule BAO-03)".
- **Status:** a report is a draft until a named teacher approves it. It is released only after that.

Each report is checked for the following before it is released:

1. Every finding names record IDs and rule numbers.
2. No finding gives a learner a trait, ranks or compares learners, or suggests a pathway.
3. Every number matches the figures worked out in section 6.
4. A named teacher has approved the report.
