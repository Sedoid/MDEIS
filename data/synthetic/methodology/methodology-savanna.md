# Savanna Academy: How We Read Learning Over Time

**Document:** SAV-METH-1.0 | **Version:** 1.0 | **Effective:** school year 2027

*Savanna Academy is a fictional school. This is a synthetic test fixture with no real school, teacher or learner in it. Every level, count and number in it is invented for testing.*

## 1. About this document

Savanna Academy does not use marks out of 100. A teacher records a level from 1 to 5 for each subject, and ticks a four-level checklist for each competence. This document says how those records are read.

It describes records. It does not decide anything about a learner, and it does not name a trait for a learner, rank or compare learners. A named teacher approves every report before it is released.

## 2. Our principles

- Levels are steps in an order. They are not numbers to add up. Savanna never averages levels.
- A learner is read against the Savanna level descriptors and against the learner's own earlier checks. Never against another learner.
- A group result has no names in it. It is shown only for a group of the size set in section 3.
- Say what the record shows, for example "level 2 in the last two checks". Never describe a learner by a trait.
- Never suggest a subject choice, a pathway or a career.
- Say plainly when there is not enough evidence.

## 3. School settings

**Institution and context:** Savanna Academy, a fictional school for Years 7 to 10. This line tells the reader which school the rules belong to.

**Analysis design:** Longitudinal for each learner, reading that learner's own checks over time. Group shares are for school development only. This line says which kinds of reading are allowed.

**Learner or group:** A single learner by pseudonymous ID, or one defined group in one school. This line says who a report can be about.

**Subjects and learning areas:** Mathematics, English, Science and Social Studies, plus the competences in section 6.1. This line lists what the rules cover.

**Learning standards:** The Savanna Level Descriptors v1.0 (fictional), one set for each subject. They say what each level looks like.

**Assessment rules:** A teacher records one level for each subject at the end of each term, and the checklist at the same time. Section 5 gives the detail.

**Calculation rules:** The measures in sections 6.2 to 6.5. There is no averaging of any kind.

**Classification rules:** The level scale and the four checklist levels in section 6.1. They name a level of work. They never name a learner.

**Comparison rules:** A learner's own levels over time in one subject only. Never across learners or across subjects.

**Reporting, roles and approvals:** Plain words, levels shown as recorded, and a draft until a named teacher approves it (section 7).

**Evidence requirements:** The minimum number of checks and the minimum coverage for each measure, as set out in sections 6.2 to 6.5.

**Level scale:** Ordinal, whole levels 1 to 5, set by the teaching staff.

**Checklist levels:** Four levels in order, Starting, Practising, Confident and Independent, set by the teaching staff.

**Share level:** 3 or above, set by the head teacher and the teaching staff.

**Minimum group size:** 10 learners in the group, set by the head teacher and the teaching staff.

**Step change window:** The last 4 recorded levels in one subject, set by the teaching staff.

**Step change threshold:** 2 levels, set by the teaching staff.

**Minimum checks for a step change:** 3 recorded levels, set by the teaching staff.

**Minimum checks for a sustained level:** 2 recorded checks, set by the teaching staff.

**Minimum coverage:** 75% of the learners in the group, set by the head teacher and the teaching staff.

## 4. How a check is made

The measures in section 6 are applied when a teacher asks for a report on a learner or on a group. The steps are:

1. Name the question: one learner or one group, the subject, and the checks it covers.
2. Confirm the settings in section 3 and that a named teacher is signed in as the approver.
3. Collect the recorded levels. A check with no level is "not recorded". It is never level 1 and never zero.
4. Validate each level. A level outside 1 to 5 is not used and goes to the teacher as a question.
5. Work out the measure that fits the question: section 6.2, 6.3, 6.4 or 6.5.
6. Write the finding in plain words with its record IDs and rule numbers.
7. Send the draft to the named teacher for approval.

## 5. Records we keep

A check is one recording of a level, made by the teacher at the end of a term. Each check keeps the check ID, the learner ID, the subject, the date and the level from 1 to 5. A competence check keeps the competence and one of the four checklist levels.

A level is a judgement of the work against the Savanna Level Descriptors. The teacher owns it. The system records it exactly as given and never changes it.

## 6. Levels and measures

Levels are counted and compared in order. They are never added up and never averaged.

### 6.1 The scales

The level scale for each subject:

- Level 1: shows the first steps of the skill, with direct help.
- Level 2: shows the skill in familiar tasks, with some help.
- Level 3: meets the standard of the Year, working alone.
- Level 4: meets the standard in new tasks.
- Level 5: meets the standard in new tasks and explains it to others.

The four checklist levels for a competence, in order: Starting, Practising, Confident, Independent. A checklist level is shown by its name. It is never turned into a number and never combined with a subject level.

### 6.2 Share of learners at level 3 or above

**Inputs.** The recorded levels of every learner in one group, for one subject and one check, together with the share level and the minimum group size in section 3.

**Rule.** Share = learners at level 3 or above ÷ learners with a recorded level × 100, rounded to a whole number. A half rounds up.

The group must have at least 10 learners. Below that, no share is shown and the report says the group is too small. Both values are set by the school and listed in section 3. No names are shown, and one school only is covered.

**Worked example.** A group of 25 learners has a recorded level for all 25. Of these, 17 are at level 3 or above. 17 ÷ 25 × 100 = 68, so the share is 68%, shown as "17 of 25". A second group of 12 has 7 at level 3 or above. 7 ÷ 12 × 100 = 58.33, which rounds to 58%.

### 6.3 Step change

**Inputs.** One learner's recorded levels in one subject, in date order. The window is the last 4 recorded levels. At least 3 are needed.

**Rule.** Step change = the last level minus the first level in the window, counted in levels. A rise is a step change of +2 or more. A fall is a step change of -2 or less. Any other step change is reported as no step change. The step change threshold is 2 levels, set by the teaching staff and listed in section 3.

**Worked example.** The levels in Science, oldest first, were 2, 2, 3 and 4. The last level minus the first level is 4 minus 2, which is +2, so it is a rise. A second window, 2, 3, 3 and 3, gives +1, which is no step change.

### 6.4 Sustained level

**Inputs.** The last two recorded levels of a learner in one subject, or the last two recorded checklist levels of one competence, in their order.

**Rule.** A level is defined as sustained when the last check is at the same level as the check before it, or at a higher level. It is not sustained when the last check is lower. Two recorded checks are needed. This number is set by the teaching staff and listed in section 3.

**Worked example.** Levels 3 then 4 are sustained, because the later level is higher. Levels 4 then 3 are not sustained, because the later level is lower. For a competence, Practising then Confident is sustained.

### 6.5 Evidence coverage

**Inputs.** The number of learners in the group, and the number of those with a recorded level for the subject and the check.

**Rule.** Coverage = learners with a recorded level ÷ learners in the group × 100, rounded to a whole number. The minimum coverage is 75%, set by the school and listed in section 3. Below the minimum, the share in section 6.2 is still shown, with the words "limited: coverage is below the minimum" and the coverage figure. Coverage describes the records. It does not describe achievement.

**Worked example.** A group of 20 learners has a recorded level for 14. 14 ÷ 20 × 100 = 70, which is below 75, so the result is limited. A group of 16 with 12 recorded gives 75, which meets the minimum.

## 7. Reporting and approval

Say what the record shows, in the school's wording. For example: "Science, levels 2, 2, 3 and 4 over four checks, a rise of 2 levels (records C-0001 to C-0004, rule SAV-03)". Colour and tone stay neutral.

Each report is checked for these points before it is released:

1. Every finding names record IDs and rule numbers.
2. No finding gives a learner a trait, ranks or compares learners, or suggests a pathway.
3. Every count and share matches the figures worked out in section 6.
4. A named teacher has approved the report.

A report is a draft until the named teacher approves it.

## 8. Rule register

- **SAV-01** Share at level 3 or above, for a group of at least 10 learners, no names (section 6.2).
- **SAV-02** Evidence coverage of at least 75%, otherwise the result is limited (section 6.5).
- **SAV-03** Step change: a rise of 2 levels or more, a fall of 2 levels or more, otherwise none (section 6.3).
- **SAV-04** Sustained level: the last check at the same level or higher (section 6.4).
- **SAV-05** A check with no level is not recorded and is never level 1.
- **SAV-06** No averaging of levels, no trait named for a learner, no ranking, no pathway.
- **SAV-07** A report is a draft until a named teacher approves it.

## 9. Worked test cases

These worked test cases were worked out by hand from the rules above.

**SV-01**
Scenario: Mathematics, a group of 25 learners, a recorded level for all 25, 17 at level 3 or above
Expected: Share 68% (17 of 25). Coverage 100%, so no limit

**SV-02**
Scenario: English, a group of 12 learners, a recorded level for all 12, 7 at level 3 or above
Expected: Share 58% (7 of 12, which is 58.33 rounded). Coverage 100%

**SV-03**
Scenario: Science, a group of 9 learners, a recorded level for all 9
Expected: No share is shown. The group is below the minimum group size of 10, and the report says it is too small

**SV-04**
Scenario: Social Studies, a group of 20 learners, a recorded level for 14, 9 of them at level 3 or above
Expected: Share 64% (9 of 14, which is 64.29 rounded). Coverage 70%, below the minimum of 75%, so the result is limited and says so

**SV-05**
Scenario: Mathematics, a group of 16 learners, a recorded level for 12, 6 of them at level 3 or above
Expected: Coverage 75%, which meets the minimum. Share 50% (6 of 12), with no limit

**SV-06**
Scenario: Science, one learner, the last 4 recorded levels, oldest first: 2, 2, 3, 4
Expected: Step change +2, a rise. Last two checks 3 then 4: sustained

**SV-07**
Scenario: English, one learner, the last 4 recorded levels, oldest first: 4, 3, 3, 2
Expected: Step change -2, a fall. Last two checks 3 then 2: not sustained

**SV-08**
Scenario: Mathematics, one learner, the last 4 recorded levels, oldest first: 3, 4, 3, 3
Expected: Step change 0, so no step change. Last two checks 3 then 3: sustained

**SV-09**
Scenario: Science, one learner, 2 recorded levels, oldest first: 2, 4
Expected: Step change not stated, because 2 levels are fewer than the minimum of 3. Last two checks 2 then 4: sustained

**SV-10**
Scenario: Social Studies, one learner, the last 4 recorded levels, oldest first: 2, 3, 3, 3
Expected: Step change +1, which is below 2, so no step change. Last two checks 3 then 3: sustained

**SV-11**
Scenario: English, one learner, 5 recorded levels, oldest first: 1, 2, 2, 4, 5
Expected: Only the last 4 are used: 2, 2, 4, 5. Step change +3, a rise. Last two checks 4 then 5: sustained

**SV-12**
Scenario: Mathematics, one learner, a level recorded at check 1 (3), none at check 2, then 4 and 4
Expected: The check with no level is not recorded and is not level 1. The 3 recorded levels are 3, 4, 4. Step change +1, so no step change. Last two checks 4 then 4: sustained

**SV-13**
Scenario: Competence checklist, one learner, last two checks: Confident then Practising
Expected: Not sustained, because the later level is lower. Shown by name only, with no number

**SV-14**
Scenario: A request for the average level of a group, or of one learner across terms
Expected: Declined: Savanna does not average levels. The counts and levels are shown as recorded

**SV-15**
Scenario: A recorded level of 6
Expected: Not used, because it is outside 1 to 5. It goes to the teacher as a question for correction
