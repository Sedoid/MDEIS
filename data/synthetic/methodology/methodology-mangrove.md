# Mangrove School Evaluation Policy

**Document:** MAN-POL-0.3 | **Version:** 0.3 | **Effective:** school year 2027

*Mangrove School is a fictional school. This is a synthetic test fixture with no real school, teacher or learner in it. It is written loosely on purpose, to test the clarity gate.*

## 1. Introduction

Mangrove School wants every learner's progress to be understood fairly. This policy explains how the school looks at term results over time. Teachers, parents and the head teacher can all read it. It is meant to be helpful, and it will be improved as the school learns what works.

## 2. Our approach

We look at each learner's own results over time. We do not compare learners with each other, and we never describe a child by a trait. Results are reported as the records show them. The pass mark is 40. It is important that parents understand what a report is telling them.

## 3. School profile and settings

| Setting | Value and what it is for |
| --- | --- |
| Institution and educational context | Mangrove School, a fictional day school for learners aged 11 to 16. It says which school this policy belongs to |
| Evaluation type | Longitudinal: each learner's own results over time. It means no group results are made |
| Learner or group | One learner at a time. It says who a report is about |
| Subject or learning area | Mathematics, English and Science. It lists the subjects covered |
| Learning standards | The Mangrove syllabus for each year. It says what each year is expected to cover |
| Assessment rules | One test for each subject each term, marked out of 100 |
| Calculation rules | The measures in sections 6.2 to 6.4 |
| Reporting preferences | Short reports in plain language, signed off by a teacher before they are shared |

The school will add the remaining settings once the staff have talked them through.

## 4. How evaluation is carried out

Evaluation is done every term. Each learner's results are collected from the teachers, and a completeness check is applied when the results arrive. Results are checked for errors before they are used. A short report is prepared for each learner, and a suitable level of detail is chosen for the reader. The report goes to a teacher, who reads it and releases it to the family.

## 5. Records

Each result is kept with the learner's ID, the subject, the term and the date. Where possible, the original marked paper is kept as well. A result that looks wrong is queried with the teacher where necessary. The trend window is 3 terms. Missing results are handled in an appropriate way. Results that arrive late are added as needed.

## 6. Calculations

This section describes the three measures the school reports. Each one is worked out for a single learner in a single subject.

### 6.1 Results and levels

Results are marked out of 100. The pass mark is 45. A result at or above the pass mark is described as meeting the standard, and a result below it as not yet meeting the standard.

### 6.2 Level of a result

The level of a result is worked out as the band that holds the result, using the level table kept by the school office. Each band has a score range, and the threshold for moving to the next band is the bottom of that range. For example, a result of 72 sits in the band that starts at 70.

### 6.3 Progress

Progress is defined as the last result minus the first result in the period. A rise is shown as a positive figure and a fall as a negative figure. The report states the figure and the subject it belongs to.

### 6.4 Trend

The trend is worked out as the direction of the results in the trend window, using one result for each term. For instance, results that go up in every term show a rising trend. The trend window is 4 terms, and a trend is stated when enough results exist.

## 7. Reporting

Reports are written in plain language. A report describes the results and the trend, and it says when there is too little evidence. Reports are checked for clarity before they are shared with parents. A reasonable amount of time is allowed for the teacher to read each draft. It is important to record who read the report.

## 8. Roles and review

The head teacher looks after this policy. Subject teachers read the reports for their own subjects. Where feasible, a second teacher reads the report too. Parents may ask questions about a report where necessary, and the school will answer them. The policy is reviewed each year.
